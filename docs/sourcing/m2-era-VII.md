## Proposed scenes (8)

> **Superseded** by "Era VII expanded" at the end of this note (owner, 2026-11-13). Kept for reference.

Treatment: VHS, toned down for this era (see D48).

**VII-1 · Drenica, February–March 1998.**
On 28 February and 1 March 1998 Serbian police attacked the villages of Likoshan and Qirez in
Drenica. Ten members of the Ahmeti family were executed, and four Sejdiu brothers died in police
custody. *Sources: HRW 1998; HRW chronology; Judah.* [verify: the names and numbers in HRW's own
text; HRW and later accounts give different totals for the two villages, so the caption names the
families and gives no overall total]

**VII-2 · Prekaz, 5–7 March 1998.**
Serbian police and special forces besieged the family compound of Adem Jashari, a founder of the
UÇK, in Prekaz. Adem Jashari and almost his entire family were killed; an eleven-year-old girl,
Besarta, survived. The killings drove thousands of young Albanians to join the UÇK.
*Sources: HRW 1998 ("Jashari's entire family, save an eleven-year-old girl, was killed");
Judah; Elsie.* [verify: the number killed, commonly given as 58, including women and children;
the survivor's name] → D50.

**VII-3 · The offensive of 1998.**
Through the summer and autumn of 1998 Serbian forces shelled and burned villages across Kosovo.
Hundreds of thousands of Albanians were driven from their homes, many into the forests and
mountains. In October 1998 an agreement between US envoy Richard Holbrooke and Milošević brought
unarmed OSCE monitors, the Kosovo Verification Mission, into Kosovo.
*Sources: HRW 1998; UNHCR; OSCE; Judah.* [verify: UNHCR's displacement figure (about 300,000 by
autumn 1998); the date of the agreement (13 October 1998)]

**VII-4 · Reçak, 15 January 1999.**
In the village of Reçak, Serbian forces killed at least 45 Albanians. The head of the OSCE
mission, William Walker, went to the village the next day and called it a massacre. The ICTY later
found that at least 45 Albanians were killed in the operation, many shot at close range.
*Sources: ICTY Đorđević (findings on Račak/Reçak); HRW (29 January 1999); OSCE.* [verify: the
exact wording of the ICTY finding] → D44.

**VII-5 · Rambouillet, February–March 1999.**
At peace talks in Rambouillet, near Paris, the Albanian delegation signed the proposed agreement
on 18 March 1999. The Yugoslav delegation refused.
*Sources: Judah; IICK; Elsie.* [verify dates]

**VII-6 · 24 March 1999.**
On 24 March 1999 NATO began air strikes against Yugoslavia. In the same days Serbian forces
launched a campaign of killing and mass expulsion against Kosovo's Albanians. Among the places
where civilians were killed in the first weeks: Krusha e Madhe and Krusha e Vogël, Suhareka,
Izbica, Gjakova, Poklek. *Sources: ICTY Milutinović; ICTY Đorđević; HRW Under Orders.*
[verify: each place and date against the ICTY findings] → D44, D45, D46.

**VII-7 · The exodus.**
In the weeks that followed, more than 800,000 Albanians were driven out of Kosovo into Albania,
Macedonia and Montenegro; hundreds of thousands more were displaced inside Kosovo. Refugees were
sent across the borders by train and on foot, and many had their identity papers taken. Albanian
families and the diaspora took in refugees; camps rose at Kukës, Stenkovec and elsewhere. On 27
April 1999 Serbian forces killed hundreds of Albanian men around Meja, near Gjakova.
*Sources: HRW Under Orders (862,979 expelled); UNHCR (about 800,000 fled or were expelled);
ICTY Milutinović; KMB.* [verify: the Meja figure: HLC counts 352 civilians, other counts differ;
the caption says "hundreds"] → D43, D44.

**VII-8 · June 1999: the end of the war.**
On 9 June 1999 the military agreement of Kumanovo was signed, and on 10 June the UN Security
Council adopted Resolution 1244. NATO-led troops, KFOR, entered Kosovo on 12 June, and within weeks
most of the refugees came home. In all, about 10,000 Albanians had been killed or were missing.
More than a thousand of the missing have still not been found.
*Sources: UNSC Resolution 1244; KMB; ICRC; IICK.* [verify: the Albanian figure in the current KMB
breakdown, see D42; the number still missing among Albanians] → D42.

## Decisions for Era VII

**D42 · How many were killed.**
The Humanitarian Law Center's *Kosovo Memory Book* lists every person killed or missing in
1998–2000 by name, with circumstances. It was evaluated independently by statisticians (HRDAG,
2014), who found it documents "all or nearly all" deaths. It is the most solid count of any war in
the former Yugoslavia. Its total is about 13,500 people of all ethnicities; an older published
breakdown gave 8,693 Albanians killed, with Albanians also forming most of the missing.
[verify the current Albanian figure, killed and missing]
*Recommendation:* use the KMB figure for Albanians, attributed: *"About 10,000 Albanians were
killed or went missing (Humanitarian Law Center)."* The exact number goes on screen once checked.
This is the rare case where a figure is well established, so D8 does not stop it.

**D43 · How many were expelled.**
HRW counted 862,979 Albanians expelled; UNHCR wrote that some 800,000 fled or were expelled.
*Recommendation:* "more than 800,000", attributed. The two main sources agree closely.

**D44 · Which killings to name, and with which numbers.**
There were dozens of mass killings. The ICTY judgments fix minimum figures for many of them.
*Recommendation:* name the places and dates (as in VII-1, VII-4, VII-6, VII-7). Give a number only
where an ICTY judgment fixes it ("at least 45" at Reçak), otherwise "dozens" or "hundreds".
**No photographs of the dead or wounded**, anywhere in the era.

**D45 · Sexual violence.**
HRW documented rape by Serbian forces as part of the expulsions. No reliable count exists;
figures of about 20,000 are estimates.
*Recommendation:* one sentence in VII-6 or VII-7, attributed to HRW, with no number:
*"Serbian forces also used rape against Albanian women, Human Rights Watch documented."* The
survivors' later recognition (Kosovo's 2014 law) belongs in Era IX.

**D46 · Civilians killed by NATO bombs.**
HRW counted about 500 civilian deaths from NATO bombing across Yugoslavia. Among them were
Albanian refugees: convoys on the road near Gjakova (Meja–Bistrazhin) on 14 April 1999, and the
village of Korisha on 14 May 1999. [verify the numbers in HRW NATO, Appendix B]
*Recommendation:* one clause in VII-6 or VII-7: *"NATO's bombs also killed civilians, among them
Albanian refugees on the road near Gjakova and at Korisha."* Your call.

**D47 · The trials of UÇK leaders.**
On 16 September 2026 the Kosovo Specialist Chambers in The Hague (a court of Kosovo's own law,
set up in 2015 at the EU's request) convicted four former UÇK leaders, Hashim Thaçi, Kadri
Veseli, Rexhep Selimi and Jakup Krasniqi, of war crimes against detainees in 1998–99; most of the
victims were Albanians accused of collaborating or opposing the UÇK, others were Serbs and Roma.
The verdict is first-instance and can be appealed. ICTY trials of UÇK commanders (Limaj,
Haradinaj) ended earlier in acquittals or, in one case, a conviction (Haradin Bala).
This is a new decision, not D9/D21: most of the victims were Albanians, and it is the most widely
reported Kosovo story of 2026, so international visitors will know it.
*Recommendation:* one factual sentence in **Era IX** (where the court and the verdict belong), not
in Era VII, noting that it can be appealed. Your call; I raise it once.

**D48 · How the war era looks and sounds.**
Your brief asks for restraint. *Recommendation:* the VHS look without its showy parts (no tracking
glitches or colour bleed, only grain and a slight softness), **no camcorder date stamp anywhere in
Era VII** (even on real dated photos, a date stamp over refugees turns their suffering into an
effect), and silence or a very low hum for sound. The chapter-change card stays as it is.

**D49 · Photographs of people.**
Many 1999 photos show identifiable refugees, including children, at the worst moment of their
lives. *Recommendation:* prefer wide shots (columns of people, camps, roads, the return) over
close-ups of distressed faces; never the dead or wounded.

**D50 · Adem Jashari.**
In Kosovo he is officially honoured as the "Legendary Commander" (Komandanti Legjendar).
*Recommendation:* *"Adem Jashari, a founder of the UÇK, honoured in Kosovo as the Legendary
Commander"* in the caption. The facts of the siege are stated as HRW recorded them.

**D51 · The name for Milošević's campaign.**
Some sources call the 1999 expulsions "Operation Horseshoe", after a plan whose authenticity is
disputed. *Recommendation:* don't use the name; describe what the ICTY found (a campaign of
killing and mass expulsion).

**D52 · What happens after 12 June 1999.**
The violence against Serbs and Roma after the war, and the UN administration, belong to Era VIII.
Under D9/D21 the former is not included; *recommendation:* the Era VIII note proposes how to begin
the UNMIK years without it, and asks you then.

### 1998

**1 · Likoshan and Qirez (28 February – 1 March).** As VII-1 above.

**2 · Prishtina, 2 March.** Tens of thousands protested in Prishtina against the killings in
Drenica. Police broke up the march with tear gas and water cannon and beat protesters; journalists
were beaten too. *Sources: HRW (2 March 1998); Amnesty 1998.* [verify the size of the crowd]

**3 · Prekaz (5–7 March).** As VII-2 above, with D50.

**4 · Bread for Drenica (16 March).** Women from Prishtina set out on foot carrying bread to the
besieged villages of Drenica. Police blocked them at Fushë Kosova. The women's protests of March
1998 brought the world's attention to Drenica.
*Sources: OHK ("Bread for Drenica Women's March"); Periskopi (Edita Tahiri).* [verify the date and
where the march was stopped]

**5 · The UÇK grows (spring–summer).** After Prekaz thousands of volunteers joined the UÇK; villages
organised their own defence. The UÇK fought in Rahovec (July) and held Junik under siege until
August. Among those who fell that year were Fehmi and Xhevë Lladrovci, husband and wife.
*Sources: Perritt; Pettifer; Judah; Elsie.* [verify every date and name]

**6 · The 1998 offensive.** As VII-3 above (villages burned, hundreds of thousands displaced,
families in the forests).

**7 · Abri e Epërme and Golluboc (26 September).** In the woods of Abri e Epërme, Serbian forces
killed 21 members of the Deliaj family, among them women and children; at nearby Golluboc men were
executed. *Sources: HRW Drenica; HRW 1998.* [verify: 21 in Abri e Epërme; the number at Golluboc
(13 in HRW)]

**8 · The OSCE monitors (October).** The Holbrooke–Milošević agreement and the Kosovo Verification
Mission, as in VII-3.

**9 · The diaspora.** Albanians in Germany, Switzerland, the US and elsewhere marched in the
streets of their cities, paid into the "Homeland Calling" (Vendlindja Thërret) fund, and some
went to fight, among them a volunteer unit from the United States, "Atlantiku".
*Sources: Perritt; Judah.* [verify: the demonstrations (cities, dates, sizes) in the Swiss and
German press of 1998–99; the fund's dates; the Atlantiku unit] Photos: the diaspora's own photo
archives (permission route).

### 1999

**10 · Reçak (15 January).** As VII-4 above (ICTY: at least 45 killed).

**11 · Rambouillet (February–March).** As VII-5 above.

**12 · 24 March: NATO.** NATO began air strikes against Yugoslavia under its commander, US
General Wesley Clark. *Sources: IICK; Judah.* Photo: Wesley Clark, 1999 (public domain).

**13 · Krusha e Madhe, Krusha e Vogël, Bellacërka (25–26 March).** In the villages around Rahovec,
Serbian forces separated the men from the women and children and killed them. *Sources: ICTY
Milutinović (Orahovac/Rahovec municipality); HRW Under Orders.* [verify each village's figure in
the judgment]

**14 · Suhareka (26 March).** Members of the Berisha family, most of them women and children,
were killed in a pizzeria in Suhareka. *Sources: ICTY Milutinović (Suva Reka); HRW Under Orders.*
[verify the number, usually given as over 40]

**15 · Izbica and Podujeva (28 March).** At Izbica in Drenica, men were separated from a crowd of
refugees and shot; in Podujeva, women and children of the Gashi family and others were killed.
*Sources: ICTY Milutinović (Srbica/Skenderaj); HRW Under Orders.* [verify the numbers and the
Podujeva family names]

**16 · Prishtina emptied (late March–April).** Tens of thousands of Prishtina's Albanians were
forced from their homes and put on trains to the Macedonian border. The philosopher Fehmi Agani,
Rugova's adviser, was taken from a train and killed in May. *Sources: ICTY Milutinović
(Priština); HRW Under Orders; Elsie (Agani).* [verify dates]

**17 · Gjakova (April).** In Gjakova, families were killed in their houses on Qerim Street (1–2
April), and on 27 April Serbian forces killed hundreds of men around Meja and Korenica.
*Sources: ICTY Milutinović; ICTY Đorđević; HLC (Meja).* [verify figures; caption says "hundreds"]

**18 · Koshare (from 9 April).** The UÇK broke through the border from Albania at Koshare and held
the area until the end of the war. Among those killed were the commanders Agim Ramadani and
Sali Çeku. *Sources: Pettifer; Perritt; Elsie.* [verify: dates (9 April – 10/14 June), names, and
the unit (later the 138th "Agim Ramadani" brigade)]

**19 · The exodus.** As VII-7 above (more than 800,000; Kukës, Stenkovec; families and the
diaspora taking in refugees). Photos: Jonuz Kola (Kukës, 1999), Stenkovec 1999.

**20 · The killings go on (April–May).** Poklek (17 April), the refugee convoy at Vushtrri
(2 May), Qyshk, Pavlan and Zahaq near Peja (14 May), Cuska (14 May), and the prison of Dubrava,
where inmates were killed (May). *Sources: ICTY Milutinović; ICTY Đorđević; HRW Under Orders.*
[verify every date and figure; the caption names places and dates only]

**21 · Pashtrik (late May – June).** The UÇK opened a front on Mount Pashtrik, near the Albanian
border, with NATO air support. *Sources: Pettifer; Perritt; IICK.* [verify the dates; the name
"Operation Arrow" (Operacioni Shigjeta) is used only if a published source confirms it]

**22 · June 1999: the end, and the toll.** As VII-8 above, with the figures below.
